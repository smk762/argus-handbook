# Body pose

[argus-puppet](/packages/puppet) finds the people in an image, places 133
keypoints on each, and turns the main subject's body joints into numbers: how
varied a set's poses are, whether one pose dominates it, and whether a LoRA's
outputs sit suspiciously close to its training frames' poses. It also draws
skeleton control images for a pose ControlNet. It has no Studio stage of its
own. Curator, proof and lens call it over HTTP and keep only plain values (labels,
cluster ids, scores), never keypoints.

The curator, proof, lens, forge, Studio and Cull integrations below are merged
on each package's `dev` branch and arrive with its next release.

## What puppet measures

Every measurement uses one person per image, the **primary** subject (the
largest, most confident body, or the body that curator's primary face belongs
to). Its 17 body joints are centred on the hips and scaled by torso length, so
two poses compare in "torso units" whatever the subject's size or position. A
mirrored pose counts as the same pose.

| Number | What it says |
|---|---|
| `usable` | Images with a measurable pose. One needs a confident hip and shoulder and 8 of 17 confident body joints |
| `diversity` | 0 to 1: roughly the chance that two poses drawn at random are clearly different. On the synthetic sets one pose repeated scored 0.2 or less and a broad mix 0.8 or more |
| Clusters, `largest_cluster_share` | Poses grouped by similarity (0.25 torso units: jittered copies of one pose merge, "arms out" and "arms up" stay apart), and the largest group's share of the usable poses |
| Labels | Coarse `stance` (standing, jumping, crouching, seated, lying), `arms` (up, out, down, mixed) and `facing` (front, back, left, right), from geometry rules. `unknown` when the joints a rule needs weren't confident |
| A close match | An output pose within 0.04 torso units of a training pose. It means "the same pose", not "a copied frame" |

The definitions and their calibration are in puppet's
[metrics page](/packages/puppet/metrics).

## The limits

Read these before acting on any pose number.

- **Every threshold is calibrated on synthetic poses, not real LoRA runs.** That
  covers the diversity floor (0.3), the collapse cluster share (0.75), the
  cluster cut (0.25), the close-match distance (0.04), the copying flag
  (0.1 above the training set's own baseline), and the 40% and 75% cut-offs
  Studio and Cull use. They were set on parametric stick figures with jitter
  ([calibration](/packages/puppet/metrics#calibration)). The joint-score cut that
  decides whether a joint counts (0.3) rests on two real photos. Proof's pose gate
  is off by default for this reason: confirm the thresholds on one real run
  first.
- **Close-ups and tight crops are unusable, and that is correct.** The pose is
  measured against the hips and shoulders. A head-and-shoulders portrait, a
  waist-up crop or a legs-only crop has nothing to measure against, so it is
  `unusable`, and a set made mostly of them reads **not measurable** rather than
  scoring. Each unusable image says why (`no_confident_hip`,
  `no_confident_shoulder`, `too_few_joints`, …;
  [the reasons](/packages/puppet/metrics#why-a-pose-is-unusable)). Curator,
  Studio and Cull show how many images have no usable pose. A face-centred
  portrait set gets little from puppet.
- **Pose is measured in the image plane.** One stance shot frontal and in
  profile is two poses. A set that varies only the camera angle will pass a
  diversity check.
- **The training-data terms.** The detector and pose weights (YOLOX, DWPose) are
  published under Apache-2.0, but DWPose was trained on COCO-WholeBody, whose
  dataset terms say research and non-commercial use. So was every whole-body
  model the project found. Anyone with a commercial use should make their own
  assessment ([the licence position](/packages/puppet/models#the-licence-position)).
- **GPU lease contention.** Puppet's GPU image takes its own GPU lease (kind
  `pose`) through argus-iris or the host lease. A caller that holds a lease on
  the same GPU while it waits for puppet's answer can starve it: puppet answers
  `503 GPU busy`, the caller retries a few times, then counts the images as
  failed. Running puppet on the CPU image takes no lease and avoids this
  (measured cost on the CPU: [models](/packages/puppet/models#measured-cost)).
- **The pose library's age check fails closed.** See
  [below](#control-images-and-the-pose-library).

## Where it fits

### Before training: pose coverage

With `ARGUS_CURATOR_PUPPET_URL` set, every curator scan poses each image that
passed the earlier checks and records `pose_check` (`ok`, `unusable`,
`no_person`, `failed`), `pose_labels` and a scan-level `pose_cluster` per image,
and a `pose` block in the scan summary (diversity, cluster shares, how many
images were usable). Manifest 2.7 carries the labels and cluster to the export.
See curator's [body pose page](https://github.com/smk762/argus-curator/blob/dev/docs/body-pose.md).
The stage never fails a scan.

Three places read those fields:

- **Studio** (`/curate`, Selection coverage) shows a Body pose block: the
  selection's stance, arms and facing, the share of its most common pose cluster
  with that cluster's most central image, and how many selected images have no
  usable pose and why. One pose at 40% or more of at least 10 clustered images is
  marked as a coverage gap, 75% as a strong one.
- **Cull** (Evaluate keep set) adds a note when one pose cluster holds 40% or
  more of the kept frames that have one, and says the keep set has collapsed
  onto it at 75%.
- **forge** warns when 75% or more of the posed training images (at least 10)
  share one cluster, naming the pose. The warning changes no config.

A concept LoRA trained on 30 versions of the same jump shot learns the jump
shot. This is the place to catch it: add other poses, or accept it knowingly.

### After training: pose collapse and copying

With `PROOF_PUPPET_URL` set, proof poses a run's outputs and adds a pose block to
the eval report: diversity, the largest cluster and its medoid image, label
coverage. When the training export is readable on the same host it poses the
training frames too and compares the two sets.

```bash
argus-proof gate eval_report.json --min-pose-diversity 0.3 --max-pose-cluster-share 0.75
```

- The gate is **off by default**. Turned on, it first checks the run is
  measurable (at least 10 usable poses, half the run usable, 80% of pairs
  comparable);
  an unmeasured run never passes.
- Diversity under the floor, or one cluster at or over the share, fails with the
  run-level code `pose_collapse`, naming the pose the outputs collapsed onto.
- Pose **copying** (outputs closer to training poses than training frames are to
  each other) is a **flag for review, never a reject**. A pose-conditioned
  generation reproduces training poses by design. The close pairs point the leak
  and identity checks at the frames worth comparing.

See proof's [README](https://github.com/smk762/argus-proof/blob/dev/README.md#ci-acceptance-gate).

### Captions that agree with the pose

With `LENS_POSE_URL` set, lens's reconcile step checks posture words against
puppet's measured stance. A word the measurement contradicts is corrected (when
the tags agree with the measurement) or dropped: "a woman sitting on a bench"
becomes "a woman on a bench" when she was measured standing. A pose word is never
added. With nobody or more than one person in frame, or an `unknown` stance,
puppet abstains and the caption stays as written. See lens's
[reconciliation section](https://github.com/smk762/argus-lens/blob/dev/README.md#reconciliation-colourposeheadcounttext-fixes).

### Control images and the pose library

`argus-puppet render` (or `POST /render`) draws a pose as an OpenPose-style
skeleton PNG at the generation size, for a pose ControlNet in your ComfyUI
workflow. A pose library is built from a folder of images you're entitled to use,
deduplicated and clustered, and `library sample` picks a batch of mutually
distant poses, so a generation run covers several poses rather than one. See
puppet's [conditioning page](/packages/puppet/conditioning). Darkroom and mint
don't take a pose input yet.

`library build --age-check` sends each candidate image to curator's
`POST /faces?body=true` (`ARGUS_PUPPET_AGE_URL`) and keeps it only when **every**
face and body reads 18 or over. It fails closed: an image curator can't vouch
for, or one it can't reach curator about, is left out, and if nothing could be
checked no library is written. Without the flag and the URL nothing is sent. Use
it whenever the sources are real photographs
([the age fail-safe](/packages/puppet/index#privacy-and-the-age-fail-safe)).

## Running it

Puppet serves HTTP on port **8220**, from two images:
`ghcr.io/smk762/argus-puppet:latest` (ONNX Runtime on the CPU) and `:latest-gpu`
(CUDA). The images ship no weights. Fetch the four pinned models (about 310 MB,
each checked against its SHA-256) into the model volume once:

```bash
docker volume create puppet-models
docker run --rm -v puppet-models:/models ghcr.io/smk762/argus-puppet:latest argus-puppet models fetch
docker run -d --name argus-puppet -p 127.0.0.1:8220:8220 -v puppet-models:/models ghcr.io/smk762/argus-puppet:latest
```

There is no authentication: keep the port on loopback or behind a proxy you
control. Point the callers at it:

| Package | Variable | What it turns on |
|---|---|---|
| argus-curator | `ARGUS_CURATOR_PUPPET_URL` | The body-pose scan stage (`--no-body-pose` opts a scan out) |
| argus-proof | `PROOF_PUPPET_URL` | The eval report's pose block; the gate is a separate opt-in |
| argus-lens | `LENS_POSE_URL` | The reconcile pose verifier |
| argus-puppet | `ARGUS_PUPPET_AGE_URL` | Curator's URL, for `library build --age-check` only |

Use `http://<GPU_HOST>:8220` (or the compose service name) when puppet runs on
another machine. Studio's compose doesn't run puppet yet. Images, every
`ARGUS_PUPPET_*` variable, the data root for folder scans and GPU sharing are in
puppet's [deploy page](/packages/puppet/deploy).
